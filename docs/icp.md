# Gesso — Ideal Customer Profile & Jobs-to-be-Done

## Primary ICP: the art-curious lapsed museum-goer
Adults (broadly 25–55) who **love art but rarely engage with it day-to-day**. They visited museums in college or on trips, follow an art account or two, feel a pang walking past a gallery — but "learning art history" reads as intimidating, expensive, or something they'll do "someday." They already have a daily-game habit (Wordle, Connections, a crossword) and a commute/coffee slot they fill with their phone.

**They are not experts.** The product must never feel like an exam. Their fear is looking dumb; their delight is the small "oh, *that's* what I was looking at" moment.

### Demographics / context
- Smartphone-first, casual sessions (2–5 min), often the same time each day.
- Values: curiosity, self-improvement without pressure, aesthetics, and — increasingly — *ethical* institutions (they've read the Benin Bronzes headlines).
- Adjacent habits: NYT Games, Duolingo streaks, podcasts, Letterboxd-style "tracking my taste."

## Jobs to be Done
1. **Functional:** "Give me a tiny daily ritual that makes me a little more culturally literate — without a course, a fee, or feeling stupid."
2. **Emotional:** "Help me feel the wonder of a museum from my couch, and feel my eye getting sharper."
3. **Social:** "Give me something worth sharing that signals curiosity, not showing off."

Gesso wins when a novice finishes a daily and thinks *"I actually learned something, and it was fun"* — not *"I got a bad score."*

## Why this ICP (over the alternatives)
- **vs. hardcore trivia/daily-game crowd:** larger, stickier, and aligned with the teaching layer that is our moat. The trivia crowd will churn on difficulty; the art-curious stay for the *learning*.
- **vs. students/educators (edtech):** a real secondary market (and a monetization path), but a smaller top-of-funnel and a heavier product (LMS, classrooms). Serve them later; don't let the roadmap bend to them yet.

## What this implies for the product
| Area | Implication |
|---|---|
| **Onboarding** | First 60s must reward, not test. Lead with an *icon* everyone knows, make the reveal (the teaching moment) the hero, de-emphasize the score for cold users. |
| **Difficulty** | Default cold users to the easy tier's recognizable icons; a "not sure? here's what to look for" foothold so no one is stranded. |
| **Voice** | Warm, curious, non-gatekeeping. The Context callouts and honesty framing *are* on-brand for this ICP. |
| **Growth** | Art/museum communities, SEO on famous works, and a share artifact that signals curiosity (not a raw score). |
| **Retention** | Streak + "your eye is improving" (learning progress) beats leaderboard rank for this segment. |

## Evidence plan (close the "no user research" gap)
Cheap, credible, portfolio-worthy:
1. **5 problem interviews** with art-curious non-experts (friends-of-friends, r/museums, art-account followers): current habits, last museum visit, what stops them from "learning art," reaction to a daily.
2. **A one-question in-app survey** on first reveal: *"How did that feel — taught me something / too hard / too easy?"* (fires `activation_sentiment`).
3. **Instrument the funnel** (see `metrics.md`) and read D1/D7 by cohort.
4. Synthesize into a one-line validated problem statement and a persona card.

## One-line positioning (for this ICP)
**"The daily habit that teaches you to *see* — a two-minute museum, every morning."**
